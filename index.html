Understand variance before you pick your schedule

In MTTs, being profitable doesn't mean making money right away. Variance is massive — even with a positive edge, you can run hundreds of tournaments in the red before your EV shows up in your balance.

  • Volume and sample size — you need a large number of tournaments for results to converge toward your true ROI.
  • Bankroll and timeframe — tournament selection depends on how much you have and when you need to cash out.
  • Smaller fields = more predictable — fewer players means shorter swings; large fields require a bigger roll and more time.

    How this works

    Monte Carlo simulation using real GGPoker payout structures (freezeouts, non-PKO). Each tournament uses published buy-in, fee, and typical field size. Payouts follow standard online ladders (~12–15% ITM, top-heavy).

    The simulator adjusts finish-position odds for your ROI and runs 5,000 scenarios. Being +EV doesn't guarantee profit over a short sample — especially in 5,000+ player fields where one deep run changes everything.

    Rule of thumb: small bankroll or short timeframe → smaller fields and lower buy-ins. Large roll and long horizon → you can absorb the variance of the majors.
